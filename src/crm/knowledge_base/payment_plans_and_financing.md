# AutoMark Real Estate — Payment Plans & Financing Guide

## Overview

Buying property in Egypt does not require full cash payment. Most developers offer instalment plans with low down payments and 0% interest, making real estate highly accessible. This guide explains all financing options available.

---

## Developer Payment Plans (Most Common)

Developer payment plans are the most popular financing method in Egypt. You pay directly to the developer — no bank involvement, no interest.

### How It Works
1. Pay a **down payment** (مقدم) — typically 5% to 20% of the total price
2. Pay a **booking fee** (رسوم حجز) at signing — typically EGP 25,000–100,000 (deducted from down payment)
3. Pay quarterly or semi-annual instalments over 4–10 years
4. Take **delivery** of the unit (usually after 2–4 years of construction)
5. Continue paying remaining instalments post-delivery (on some plans)

### Bank Mortgage Requirements
- Egyptian national ID (or residence permit for foreigners)
- Proof of income (3–6 months payslips or tax return)
- Down payment: minimum 20–25% of property value
- Property must have a registered title deed (طوابو)
- Property value must be supported by bank appraisal
- Age: 21–60 years old (loan must be repaid before age 65)

### Government Social Housing Mortgage Initiative
The Egyptian government (through CBE — Central Bank of Egypt) offers subsidised mortgages for middle-income Egyptians:
- Properties up to EGP 2,500,000 may qualify
- Interest rate: 3% for low-income, 8% for middle-income
- Contact the Housing Ministry or any partnered bank for eligibility

## Resale Property Financing

When buying a resale property (from another owner, not the developer):

### Option 1: Transfer Seller's Instalment Plan
- If the seller still has outstanding instalments, you can take over the payment plan
- You pay the seller the difference between total price and remaining balance
- Requires developer approval — usually costs a transfer fee (2–5%)

### Option 2: Bank Mortgage on Resale
- Property must have a registered title deed
- Bank appraises the property and lends up to 75–80% of value
- You pay the remaining 20–25% as down payment

### Option 3: Full Cash
- Fastest and most negotiation leverage — sellers offer 5–15% discount for cash

## Foreign Buyers — Can Foreigners Buy Property in Egypt?

Yes. Foreigners can legally buy property in Egypt with the following conditions:

- **Restricted areas:** Foreigners may NOT own land in Sinai or certain border zones
- **Urban real estate:** Full ownership rights in Cairo, Alexandria, North Coast, New Capital
- **Allowed unit types:** Apartments, villas, townhouses within compounds
- **Maximum owned units:** 2 properties per foreign national (Egyptian law)
- **Payment currency:** Must be paid in foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP, SAR) and converted via official banking channels (to protect the foreign exchange record)
- **Residence benefit:** Owning property worth USD 50,000+ may qualify buyer for a renewable 3-year residence permit

### Required Documents for Foreign Buyers
- Valid passport
- Entry visa to Egypt
- Bank transfer record showing foreign currency used for purchase
- Sales contract (عقد بيع) notarised and registered

## Frequently Asked Questions — Payments

**Q: Can I pay in US dollars?**
A: Some developers accept USD, especially for North Coast and luxury properties. Most accept EGP only. Foreign buyers must show a bank transfer in foreign currency.

**Q: What happens if I miss an instalment?**
A: Most contracts allow a 30–60 day grace period. After that, a late fee of 1–2% per month may apply. Persistent default may result in contract cancellation (with partial refund per contract terms — typically 10% deducted).

**Q: Can I sell the unit while still paying instalments?**
A: Yes — this is called a "resale on plan." The new buyer takes over your instalment plan (with developer approval) and pays you the profit (your equity). Transfer fees apply (2–5% of remaining balance).

**Q: Is there a registration fee?**
A: Yes — registration (تسجيل) is 2.5% of the contract value, paid to the notary/real estate registration authority. This secures the title deed in your name.

**Q: Can I get a receipt/official invoice for each instalment?**
A: Yes — all reputable developers issue official receipts. Always keep these. They protect your ownership rights.
